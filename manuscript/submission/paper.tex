\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Background:} Nearly all existing chemical probes of the ATR--CHK1
replication-stress checkpoint are ATP-competitive kinase inhibitors. Two
2025 cryo-EM structures of the ATR kinase domain (PDB 9L40, 2.87~{\AA},
bound to berzosertib/VE-822; PDB 9L4B, 3.20~{\AA}, bound to
camonsertib/RP-3500) provide new templates for structure-based work on this
target, and companion depositions revealed berzosertib additionally
engaging an allosteric site at the ATR--ATR dimer interface, distinct from
the canonical ATP pocket. Whether a newly deposited structure is reliable
for conventional ATP-pocket docking is not something resolution alone can
guarantee. \textbf{Methods:} We assembled a small seed library of five
ATR/CHK1-directed compounds, three extracted directly from the
co-crystallized active-site ligand of PDB 9L40, 9L4B, and a CHK1 reference
structure (PDB 2YM8), generated derivatives by BRICS recombination,
filtered for developability by standard descriptor rules, and -- before
docking any candidate -- validated each receptor by redocking its own
native ligand (naive and symmetry-corrected RMSD, three seeded AutoDock
Vina replicates) against a pre-specified 2.0~{\AA} pass threshold.
\textbf{Results:} Redocking passed for 9L4B ($0.61\pm0.02$~{\AA}) and 2YM8
($0.76$--$0.90$~{\AA}) but reproducibly \emph{failed} for 9L40 (naive
$2.96\pm0.06$~{\AA}, symmetry-corrected $2.53\pm0.01$~{\AA} -- still over
threshold) despite looser-than-passing but still self-consistent search
reproducibility ($0.56$~{\AA}); notably, 9L40 is the \emph{higher}-resolution
structure of the two. Two alternative explanations were tested and ruled
out: the docking box cannot reach the nearby allosteric site
(60.65~{\AA} away), and the deposited pose is not an outlier by map-model
Q-score (though residue inclusion is somewhat lower). 9L40 was excluded
from scoring per the pre-specified rule. A 40-compound sanity-check screen
(5 seeds + 35 developability-passed derivatives) against the validated
9L4B structure recovered camonsertib -- the receptor's own ligand -- as
top-ranked, with no derivative exceeding the seed compounds.
Extending this single-structure finding, the same validated pipeline was
run on 14 additional recently deposited cryo-EM structures of an unrelated
kinase complex (the CDK7/cyclin~H/MAT1 CDK-activating kinase, CAK, each
bound to a distinct ATP-competitive inhibitor): 13/14 failed redocking
(overall $n=17$ structures, 3/17 pass), showing the phenomenon is not
specific to ATR. Across this survey, wwPDB map-model fit metrics did
\emph{not} reliably predict which structures would fail: passing
structures did not have higher Q-scores than failing ones (mean
$0.569$ vs. $0.688$, $n=12$ with a Q-score reported, if anything the
opposite of the expected direction, though only 2 of the 12 pass -- too
few for this comparison to be more than descriptive), and residue
inclusion showed no relationship ($r=-0.14$, $p=0.61$, $n=16$). Allowing
five active-site residues to
move during redocking (flexible-sidechain Vina) improved, but did not
rescue, the 9L40 pose: mean symmetry-corrected RMSD $3.00\pm0.92$~{\AA}
across three seeds, versus $4.50\pm0.05$~{\AA} in a matched rigid control
(same enlarged box and receptor preparation, no flexible residues) --
better, but no seed passed the $2.0$~{\AA} threshold. Docking the same 40-compound pilot set against the excluded 9L40
structure instead of the validated 9L4B produced a substantially different
ranking, confirmed under a matched three-seed-per-receptor design
(Spearman $\rho=0.21$, $p=0.19$ between the two receptors' scores; only
5/10 compounds shared between the two top-10 lists), demonstrating
concretely what is lost by skipping the validation step. Because the CAK
failure rate is far below AutoDock Vina's documented self-docking success
rate on curated benchmarks (58\% on the 285-complex PDBbind Core Set), the
identical, unmodified pipeline was additionally run as a positive control
on 20 objectively selected, unrelated CASF-2016 complexes: it passed 8/20
(40\%), statistically indistinguishable from the literature baseline
($p=0.08$) -- ruling out a badly broken pipeline -- while the CAK rate
remains a significant outlier even against this pipeline's own measured
40\% baseline ($p=0.008$). \textbf{Conclusions:} A newly deposited,
high-resolution structure is not automatically a reliable docking
receptor; this can only be established empirically, per structure and per
protocol, before any candidate is scored, and -- at least in this $n=17$
sample -- cannot be substituted for by a map-model fit metric alone. All
code, data, and structures are openly available.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ATR kinase; molecular docking; redocking
validation; BRICS; virtual screening; reproducibility; AutoDock Vina

\section*{Background}

The ATR--CHK1 checkpoint kinase axis is the principal sensor of DNA
replication stress: ATR is activated at stalled replication forks through
protein-mediated mechanisms (the ATRIP--TOPBP1 axis, and independently
ETAA1) rather than by occupation of its own ATP-binding pocket, and once
active it phosphorylates and activates CHK1, which restrains further origin
firing and protects replication forks from nucleolytic collapse
\citep{kumagai2006,bass2016,saldivar2017}. Genetic and pharmacological
attenuation of ATR or CHK1 activity increases instability at common fragile
sites -- chromosomal loci prone to breakage under replication stress --
consistent with this axis being protective \citep{casper2002,debatisse2012}.

Essentially all existing chemical matter directed at this pathway --
berzosertib, ceralasertib, camonsertib, prexasertib, and related compounds
-- is ATP-competitive kinase inhibitors developed for oncology
synthetic-lethality strategies \citep{fokas2012}. Two cryo-EM structures of
a homodimer of the ATR kinase domain released in 2025 -- bound respectively
to berzosertib (PDB 9L40, 2.87~{\AA}) and camonsertib (PDB 9L4B,
3.20~{\AA}) -- are a substantial improvement over the original 2018
structure of the full ATR--ATRIP complex (PDB 5YZ0, 4.7~{\AA}), and,
together with companion depositions of the complete ATR--ATRIP assembly
from the same study, newly resolved that one such assembly engages
\emph{four} berzosertib molecules -- two in the canonical ATP-competitive
active sites, and two at a previously uncharacterized allosteric pocket
formed at the ATR--ATR dimer interface \citep{wang2025}. This is a
structurally more complex picture than a single ATP-pocket story, and it
raises a practical question for anyone using these new structures for
docking: does a newly deposited, high-resolution structure automatically
make a trustworthy receptor for conventional ATP-pocket virtual screening?
As will become apparent below, the answer this pilot finds is no -- and,
notably, it is the \emph{higher}-resolution of the two structures (9L40,
2.87~{\AA}) that fails.

This is an empirical question, not one that follows from resolution or
recency, and it is the question this pilot study is organized around. Before
screening any candidate against 9L40 or 9L4B, we validate each structure's
suitability as a docking receptor by redocking its own co-crystallized
ligand -- the standard control for docking-protocol accuracy, following the
convention established on the Astex Diverse Set \citep{hartshorn2007} and
implemented here with AutoDock Vina \citep{trott2010} -- and only proceed to
score novel candidates against a structure that passes. We then report a
small pipeline sanity-check screen of BRICS-derived \citep{degen2008}
candidates against whichever structure(s) pass, as a demonstration of the
validate-before-screening principle applied to a concrete, currently topical
target.

\subsection*{Scope}

This is explicitly a pilot study, not a comprehensive virtual screening
campaign. Docking, by itself, estimates predicted binding and cannot
establish whether a ligand activates, inhibits, or otherwise modulates
ATR or CHK1 -- physiological ATR activation depends on upstream protein
interactions absent from a static structural model
\citep{kumagai2006,bass2016}. All results below are reported as predicted
binding and prioritization, not as demonstrated pharmacology. Molecular
dynamics and MM-GBSA relative energetic estimation, and a broader
cross-kinase selectivity panel, were part of the original design of the
computational pipeline this pilot draws on, but are not included here:
they require substantially more compute (GPU-accelerated MD) and additional
validated receptor structures than were available for this pilot, and are
explicitly left as future work (see Limitations) rather than run at a scale
too small to be informative.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Receptor structures}

Three receptor structures were obtained, in mmCIF format, via the
Registry of Open Data on AWS mirror of the RCSB PDB archive
(\url{s3://pdbsnapshots}, snapshot 2026-01-01), since the primary RCSB PDB
web/FTP endpoints were not reachable from the compute environment used for
this study: PDB 9L40 (ATR kinase domain, homodimer, bound to
VE-822/berzosertib, 2.87~{\AA}), PDB 9L4B (ATR kinase domain, homodimer,
bound to camonsertib/RP-3500, 3.20~{\AA}), and PDB 2YM8 (human CHK1 kinase
domain
bound to a chloroisoquinolinylamino-pyrazine-carbonitrile inhibitor,
2.07~{\AA}) \citep{wang2025}. The original 2018 ATR--ATRIP structure
(PDB 5YZ0, 4.7~{\AA} overall) was not used as a docking receptor: at that
resolution, ATP-pocket side-chain positions are not reliably resolved.

For each structure, \emph{both} protein chains (the full homodimer; standard
residues only, via Biopython \citep{cock2009}) were retained as the docking
receptor, and the co-crystallized ligand of the biologically relevant
ATP-competitive active site was identified and isolated. For 9L40, four
copies of the ligand (chemical component \texttt{A1EIE}) are present per
asymmetric unit; contact analysis (heavy atoms within 4.5~{\AA} of each
protein chain) confirmed that residues A2701/B2701 each contact only their
own chain (canonical, single-monomer ATP-competitive site) while
A2702/B2702 contact both chains (the dimer-interface allosteric site
reported by \citet{wang2025}); A2701 was used as the active-site reference
ligand. The centroid-to-centroid distance from A2701 to the nearest
allosteric copy (A2702) is 60.65~{\AA} -- far outside the reach of the
22~{\AA} docking box used here (Table~\ref{tab:redocking} below), which
rules out the empty allosteric cavity as a confound for the redocking result
that follows: the docking box could not have reached it. The analogous
single-copy active-site ligands (chemical component \texttt{A1EIK} for
9L4B; \texttt{YM8} for 2YM8) were extracted directly. New-style
five-character PDB chemical-component codes overflow the fixed-width
residue-name column of the legacy PDB format and silently corrupt naive
PDB-to-PDBQT conversion (each atom is parsed as an isolated one-atom
fragment); ligand records were therefore rewritten with a placeholder
three-character residue name before conversion. Proteins and ligands were
converted to PDBQT with Open Babel \citep{oboyle2011}, and docking search
boxes were centered on each active-site ligand's heavy-atom centroid
(22~{\AA} cubic box).

To assess whether the deposited ligand pose itself is well supported by the
experimental map -- an alternative explanation for a redocking failure that
is independent of the docking protocol -- we retrieved the official wwPDB
validation report (XML) for 9L40 and 9L4B from the same AWS mirror. For the
active-site ligand copy used as the redocking reference in each structure,
the real-space correlation-based Q-score \citep{pintilie2020} and fractional
residue inclusion (the proportion of the ligand's atoms with adequate
density support) were: 9L40/A2701, Q-score 0.553, residue inclusion 0.818;
9L4B/A2701, Q-score 0.528, residue inclusion 0.967. The Q-scores are
comparable (if anything marginally higher for 9L40), so the deposited
berzosertib pose is not obviously a poorly resolved outlier by that metric;
residue inclusion is nonetheless visibly lower for 9L40, indicating a
somewhat less complete density match for that pose. This does not, by
itself, explain a 2+~{\AA} redocking discrepancy, but it is reported here
in full rather than omitted, since it bears on how confidently the
redocking result should be interpreted (see Discussion). For reference, the
two allosteric-site copies in 9L40 (A2702, B2702) score
markedly lower still (Q-score 0.385/0.422, residue inclusion 0.364/0.485),
consistent with the dimer-interface pocket being a more weakly resolved,
peripheral region of the map.

Five compounds formed the seed library (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}): the three

and ceralasertib, whose canonical SMILES were obtained via literature search

and standardized with RDKit \citep{rdkit} (salt stripping, charge

\subsection*{Derivative generation and developability filtering}

The seed set was fragmented with the BRICS algorithm \citep{degen2008} into
22 unique fragments, recombined (\texttt{BRICS.BRICSBuild}, RDKit), and
capped at 5,000 generated candidates (a combinatorial cap, not a natural
stopping point -- see Limitations). Candidates were pre-filtered by a
relaxed Lipinski rule ($\leq$1 violation of molecular weight $\leq$500,
cLogP $\leq$5, H-bond donors $\leq$5, H-bond acceptors $\leq$10)
\citep{lipinski1997}, Veber's rule (rotatable bonds $\leq$10, TPSA
$\leq$140~{\AA}$^2$) \citep{veber2002}, and removal of any structure
matching the RDKit PAINS \citep{baell2010} or Brenk structural-alert
catalogs, leaving 786 candidates. Every retained candidate (plus the 5
seeds) was then scored against four descriptor-based drug-likeness rules
(Lipinski, Veber, Ghose \citep{ghose1999}, Egan \citep{egan2000}); 680 of
791 compounds satisfied $\geq$3/4 rules with no structural alert
(``developability-passed''). All values from this stage are rule-based
predictions, not measurements.

\subsection*{Redocking validation}

Before any candidate was scored, each receptor was validated by extracting
its own active-site ligand, redocking it into the identical receptor/box
with AutoDock Vina \citep{trott2010} (exhaustiveness 32, three independent
seeded replicates: 1000, 1001, 1002), and computing the root-mean-square
deviation (RMSD, heavy atoms) between the best-scoring pose and the native
crystallographic pose, against a pre-specified pass threshold of
$\leq$2.0~{\AA} \citep{hartshorn2007}, fixed before any receptor was tested.
Two RMSD variants are reported: a naive, index-matched RMSD (valid here
because the docked pose and the input ligand file share atom order by
construction within a single Vina run), and a symmetry-corrected RMSD
computed with spyrmsd \citep{meli2020} via graph-isomorphism atom matching,
since naive index-matched RMSD can overstate pose error for ligands with
internal topological symmetry (e.g. equivalent ring or sulfone atoms).
Before applying either metric to real poses, both were checked against two
controls: a molecule scored against its own unperturbed coordinates (must
return exactly 0.00~{\AA}), and a molecule subjected to a known, exact
1.0~{\AA} rigid translation (must return exactly 1.00~{\AA}); both controls
passed for all three ligands (Results). A receptor failing the redocking
control in any replicate is excluded from candidate scoring, with the
reason stated, rather than retried until it passes. Search reproducibility
(pairwise RMSD between replicate best poses, independent of whether that
pose is correct) was reported separately from redocking accuracy (RMSD to
the native pose), since these answer different questions.

\subsection*{Pilot library docking}

The 5 seed compounds plus the top 35 developability-passed derivatives
(ranked by number of rules satisfied, then by molecular weight ascending)
were docked against every receptor structure that passed redocking
validation, using AutoDock Vina at exhaustiveness 16 (three independent
seeded replicates: 2000, 2001, 2002) -- a lower exhaustiveness than the 32
used for redocking validation itself, for compute-cost reasons. Because the
central claim of this pilot is that a receptor must be validated under the
protocol it will actually be screened with, this discrepancy is a real
weakness in the original design, and we closed it directly rather than
assert the two protocols were equivalent: the redocking validation was
re-run in full (three seeded replicates, same seeds 1000/1001/1002) at
exhaustiveness 16, and the same pass/fail pattern held (Results) -- 9L40
still fails ($3.01\pm0.06$~{\AA}), 9L4B and 2YM8 still pass
($0.59\pm0.02$~{\AA} and $0.92\pm0.01$~{\AA}) -- so the reliability
conclusion does not depend on which of the two exhaustiveness settings is
used. Ligands were 3D-embedded and protonated at pH 7.4 with Open Babel.
The best (most negative) affinity across replicates is reported per
compound alongside the mean and standard deviation; the mean is the primary
statistic discussed in Results, since the best-of-three is a biased
(downward) estimator.

\subsection*{Multi-structure survey}

To test whether the 9L40 failure generalizes beyond a single structure, the
identical validation protocol (ligand and receptor extraction with
contact-based disambiguation of multiple ligand copies, Open
Babel-prepared PDBQTs, three seeded Vina replicates at exhaustiveness 16,
symmetry-corrected RMSD, wwPDB validation-report parsing) was packaged into
a single script and applied to 14 additional, recently deposited cryo-EM
structures identified via a literature/PDB search for the CDK7/cyclin~H/MAT1
CDK-activating kinase (CAK) complex \citep{cushing2024}, each bound to a
distinct ATP-competitive inhibitor (PDB 8P6V, 8P6W, 8P6X, 8P6Z,
8P70--8P78, 8P7L;
one entry, 8P73, has two independent bound ligand copies of different
diastereomers, of which the lower cross-chain-contact copy was used). CAK
was chosen as the extension target not because of any relationship to
ATR/CHK1 biology, but because it is an unrelated, recently deposited,
cryo-EM-solved, small-molecule-bound kinase system -- exactly the setting
needed to test whether the redocking-failure phenomenon is an ATR-specific
artifact or recurs across recent kinase depositions generally. Before
trusting any CAK-structure result, the pipeline itself was validated by
re-running it, unmodified, on 9L4B (a receptor with an independently known
redocking outcome): it reproduced the original hand-curated result almost
exactly (symmetry-corrected RMSD $0.60\pm0.03$~{\AA} vs. the original
$0.61\pm0.02$~{\AA}; Q-score $0.528$, identical to 3 decimal places;
residue inclusion $0.967$, identical to 3 decimal places), so the survey
pipeline is not a separate, unvalidated instrument. Two structures
(8P6v and 8P6w, i.e., an initial pilot pair) were additionally
cross-checked by direct inspection of the mmCIF ligand entities and
contact patterns before the full batch was run. wwPDB Q-score and residue
inclusion were extracted for each structure's bound ligand from the
official validation XML report where present; both fields are cryo-EM-only
metrics (X-ray entries such as 2YM8 report real-space correlation
coefficient/real-space R-value instead and correctly have no Q-score), and
four CAK entries have a dual-occupancy (split-altloc) ligand for which the
wwPDB pipeline itself does not report a Q-score (only residue inclusion),
which is a genuine absence in the primary data rather than an extraction
failure.

\subsection*{Positive-control validation against an external benchmark}

Because the CAK failure rate (Results) is well below AutoDock Vina's
documented self-docking success rate on curated benchmarks, the same
unmodified pipeline was additionally run on a subset of the PDBbind Core
Set (CASF-2016, 285 complexes) -- the exact benchmark against which
Vina's reported 58\% self-docking success rate is measured
\citep{zhang2023} -- as a positive control to distinguish a genuine
finding about the CAK structure series from a pipeline artifact. The
subset (20 PDB IDs) was the first 20 entries, in file order, of a
complete, externally hosted CASF-2016 core-set listing (redistributed at
\url{https://github.com/OptiMaL-PSE-Lab/DeepDock}, not curated or
filtered by us), an objective selection rule fixed before any of these
20 structures was inspected individually, to avoid any appearance of
choosing easy targets. Because CASF-2016 entries span many unrelated
protein families rather than one target's own co-crystallized ligand
list, the bound ligand of interest was not manually specified per
structure (as it was for ATR/CHK1/CAK); instead it was auto-detected as
the largest non-polymer heteroatom group remaining after excluding water
and a fixed list of common crystallization ions, buffers, and
cryoprotectants (e.g. SO4, PO4, GOL, EDO, PEG, DMS, HEPES; the complete
list was fixed before this batch was run and is in
\texttt{casf\_validation.py}). All other steps (Open Babel PDBQT
preparation, 22~{\AA} box centered on the ligand centroid, three seeded
Vina replicates at exhaustiveness 16, symmetry-corrected RMSD against the
$2.0$~{\AA} threshold) are identical to the CAK/ATR survey protocol.

\subsection*{Flexible-sidechain redocking}

As a low-compute alternative to molecular dynamics for distinguishing a
rigid-conformer docking artifact from a genuine pose/receptor problem, 9L40
was redocked with AutoDock Vina's built-in flexible-sidechain feature,
allowing five active-site residues within 4.5~{\AA} of the native
berzosertib pose (chain A: LYS2327, TYR2365, GLU2378, TRP2379, HIS2477) to
move during docking while the remainder of the receptor stayed rigid.
Rigid and flexible receptor parts were prepared with Meeko
(\path{mk_prepare_receptor.py}), which required an explicit histidine
tautomer assignment for HIS2477 (HIE) and automatic removal of $\sim$400
residues elsewhere in the structure whose side-chain density could not be
template-matched (typical of a $\sim$3~{\AA} cryo-EM map in surface/loop
regions; none of the five target flexible residues were among those
removed). The ligand was likewise prepared with Meeko
(\path{mk_prepare_ligand.py}, requiring explicit hydrogens) for
atom-typing consistency with the Meeko-prepared flexible receptor; mixing
an Open Babel-prepared ligand with a Meeko-prepared flexible receptor caused
Vina's native layer to abort rather than dock. The docking box was
enlarged from 22~{\AA} (rigid redocking) to 30~{\AA} to give the flexible
side chains room to explore alternate rotamers without a
ligand-outside-box error; three seeded replicates (1000, 1001, 1002,
exhaustiveness 32, matching the rigid protocol) were run in separate
subprocesses (concurrent in-process flexible Vina instances triggered a
crash traced to package import order between \texttt{vina} and
\texttt{spyrmsd}, not to docking itself). Because the flexible run
differs from the original rigid redocking result in three ways at once
(side-chain flexibility, a larger 30~{\AA} vs. 22~{\AA} box, and Meeko's
automatic removal of $\sim$400 template-unmatched residues from the
receptor), a matched control was additionally run: fully rigid redocking
(no residues declared flexible) with the same 30~{\AA} box and the same
Meeko-prepared receptor and ligand, isolating the effect of flexibility
alone from the other two changes.

\subsection*{Error-propagation demonstration}

To make concrete what is lost by skipping the redocking validation step,
the same 40-compound pilot set (identical SMILES, box size, and
exhaustiveness) was additionally docked against 9L40 -- the structure the
validation control excluded -- first using one seed per compound (seed
2000) and then, because a one-seed ranking is noisier than the
three-seed-averaged 9L4B ranking it was being compared against (a design
asymmetry that could depress the rank correlation on its own, independent
of any real receptor difference), re-run with the same three seeds
(2000, 2001, 2002) used for 9L4B, for a matched comparison. The resulting
per-compound rankings against 9L40 (one-seed and three-seed) were each
compared to the existing three-seed ranking against 9L4B by Spearman rank
correlation and by the overlap between each structure's top-10 compounds.

\subsection*{Computational environment}

All calculations were run with AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 (Python bindings) and
Open Babel (both via pip, CPU only, no GPU available in this environment --
see Limitations regarding molecular dynamics), RDKit 2026.03.6,
and Biopython 1.88. Receptor structures were retrieved
from the RCSB PDB AWS Open Data mirror snapshot dated 2026-01-01. All code
and intermediate data are available at the repository accompanying this
submission (see Availability of data and materials).

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Redocking validation: one of two ATR structures is unreliable
for docking}

Both RMSD sanity controls passed for all three ligands before any real pose
was evaluated: crystal-vs-itself returned exactly $0.000$~{\AA} (naive and
symmetry-corrected alike), and a deliberately applied, exact $1.0$~{\AA}
rigid translation was recovered as exactly $1.000$~{\AA} in every case.

Table~\ref{tab:redocking} summarizes the redocking validation across all
three receptor structures over three independent seeded replicates,
reporting both the naive index-matched RMSD and the symmetry-corrected
RMSD (spyrmsd \citep{meli2020}). 9L4B (ATR, camonsertib-bound, 3.20~{\AA})
and 2YM8 (CHK1) both passed with a substantial margin under either metric
(symmetry-corrected mean RMSD $0.61\pm0.02$~{\AA} and $0.76\pm0.01$~{\AA}
respectively, well under the 2.0~{\AA} threshold in every replicate). 9L40
(ATR, berzosertib-bound, 2.87~{\AA} -- the \emph{higher}-resolution of the
two ATR structures) failed reproducibly under both metrics: naive RMSD
$2.96\pm0.06$~{\AA}, symmetry-corrected $2.53\pm0.01$~{\AA}. Symmetry
correction narrows the gap -- as expected, since berzosertib has more
internal topological symmetry than camonsertib or the CHK1 ligand -- but
does not close it: $2.53$~{\AA} still exceeds the $2.0$~{\AA} threshold by
a comfortable margin, so the failure is not a symmetry artifact.

This was also not simply search noise, but the relevant comparison is
relative, not absolute: the pairwise RMSD between 9L40's own replicate best
poses (search reproducibility, $0.56$~{\AA} mean) is 12$\times$ looser than
9L4B's ($0.045$~{\AA}) and 7$\times$ looser than 2YM8's ($0.078$~{\AA}).
Read against the other two structures rather than against the 2.0~{\AA}
pass threshold, 9L40's search is both less accurate \emph{and} less
reproducible than the structures that passed -- consistent with a rougher
or flatter local score landscape in that pocket -- though it is still far
tighter than the pose-to-pose error itself, meaning Vina is converging on
a consistent answer, just not the deposited one. Two further checks rule
out two specific alternative explanations for the 9L40 failure (Methods):
the box could not have reached the nearby allosteric pocket (60.65~{\AA}
away, far outside a 22~{\AA} box), and the deposited ligand pose is not an
obvious modeling outlier by map-model fit (Q-score comparable to 9L4B's,
though with somewhat lower residue inclusion -- Methods). Per the
pre-specified rule, 9L40 was excluded from candidate scoring; the pilot
library docking below uses 9L4B only. Figure~\ref{fig:overlay} shows the
native and redocked poses for both structures directly. Because the pilot
library was screened at a lower exhaustiveness (16) than used for
validation above (32) for compute-cost reasons, we additionally re-ran the
full redocking validation at exhaustiveness 16 (Methods) to confirm this
was not a hidden inconsistency: the same pass/fail pattern held exactly --
9L40 $3.01\pm0.06$~{\AA} (fail), 9L4B $0.59\pm0.02$~{\AA} (pass), 2YM8
$0.92\pm0.01$~{\AA} (pass) -- so the reliability conclusion is not an
artifact of the exhaustiveness setting used for validation.

\subsection*{Pipeline sanity-check screen against the validated ATR structure}

This section is a sanity check on the pipeline's behavior, not a
prospective screening result: as the analysis below shows, its outcome
(the seed compound outranking its own derivatives) is close to guaranteed
by construction (see Discussion), and the compound-level ranking should be
read accordingly.

The 40-compound pilot set (5 seeds + 35 developability-passed derivatives)
was docked against 9L4B (exhaustiveness 16, three seeded replicates each;
Methods discusses the exhaustiveness used for validation vs. screening).
Best-pose predicted affinity across the set ranged from $-9.80$ to
$-7.82$ kcal/mol; leading with the more robust statistic, mean affinity
(across 3 replicates) across all 40 compounds was $-8.66\pm0.45$ kcal/mol.
Table~\ref{tab:docking} shows the top 10 ranked compounds by mean affinity.

as expected for a structure the receptor was solved with. No BRICS

closest, \path{deriv_002} ($-9.46\pm0.01$ kcal/mol), came within

this ranking should be read as indistinguishable rather than as a
near-miss. The three top-ranked derivatives (\path{deriv_002},
\path{deriv_003}, and \path{deriv_019}) share camonsertib's core
scaffold with a single substituent varied (methylamino, methoxy, or
dimethylamino in place of morpholine) -- an expected outcome of BRICS
recombination re-using the best-scoring seed compound's fragments in a new
context, rather than discovery of novel chemical matter. The 35-derivative
subset was selected by rule count then molecular weight ascending (Methods),
which systematically favors smaller ligands -- themselves typically weaker
Vina scorers -- so the pilot set is, by its own selection rule, stacked
against derivatives beating the (larger) seed compounds.

\subsection*{Multi-structure survey: redocking failure recurs, and wwPDB
map-model fit metrics do not predict it}

Table~\ref{tab:survey} reports symmetry-corrected redocking RMSD,
resolution, and wwPDB Q-score/residue inclusion for all 17 structures now
tested with this pipeline (the original 9L40/9L4B/2YM8 plus 14 CAK
structures). 14/17 (82\%) fail the 2.0~{\AA} threshold; only 9L4B, 2YM8,
and one CAK structure (8P75) pass. Failure is not confined to ATR: it
recurs across an unrelated, independently solved kinase system at a
similar or higher rate (CAK alone: 13/14, 93\%, vs. the ATR pair's 1/2,
50\%, though n=2 there). Resolution does not predict outcome (Spearman
$\rho=-0.28$, $p=0.28$, across all 17): the passing and failing
structures span an overlapping and, if anything, similarly narrow
resolution range (1.8--3.2~{\AA}, spanning both the X-ray structure 2YM8
and the cryo-EM structures; the two resolution conventions are not
strictly the same physical quantity, though with $n=17$ this distinction
changes the correlation little).

Nor do the wwPDB map-model fit metrics introduced in the previous round of
this work as a candidate explanation for the 9L40 failure. Of the 12
structures with a reported Q-score, the 2 passing structures have a
\emph{lower} mean Q-score ($0.569$) than the 10 failing structures
($0.688$) -- the opposite direction from what would be expected if a
higher Q-score meant a more reliable docking target, though with only 2
of 17 structures passing and reporting a Q-score, this is a descriptive
observation, not a correlation with an interpretable p-value, and is
reported that way rather than with a formal statistic that a 2-vs-10
split cannot support. Residue inclusion shows no relationship at all
($r=-0.14$, $p=0.61$, $n=16$, a comparison with enough passing structures
with this field reported to support a correlation coefficient). Neither
should be read as a confident refutation of the map-quality hypothesis --
but neither is the confirmation this survey set out to look for, and both
are reported as found.

8P74 and 8P75 illustrate why this survey's ``failures'' need to be read
individually, not only as a rate. They were reported in an earlier
version of this analysis as independent depositions of the same complex
bound to the same inhibitor, disagreeing on redocking outcome
independent of any property of the compound -- a claim that does not
survive checking their own deposition titles, which identify them as
\emph{ring-up} and \emph{ring-down} conformational classes of the same
flexible substituent (inhibitor ICEC0880, comp\_id X3Z), separated by the
depositors from one underlying cryo-EM dataset because the ligand
genuinely populates both conformations \citep{cushing2024}. A large RMSD
between two deliberately separated conformer models is expected, not
surprising, and this pair is not evidence of coordinate-set-specific
unpredictability. It also does not support the natural alternative
explanation -- that 8P74's docked pose is really a correct match to the
\emph{other} deposited conformer, unfairly scored as a failure against
the wrong reference: computed directly, 8P74's docked pose is
$6.76$~{\AA} from 8P75's native ligand, no closer than the $6.67$~{\AA}
from its own. Both checks are reported in full in the Discussion, along
with what they leave open.

\subsection*{Flexible-sidechain redocking: a confounded first comparison,
corrected}

Allowing the five active-site residues nearest the native berzosertib
pose to move during docking did not recover it: mean symmetry-corrected
RMSD across three seeds was $3.00\pm0.92$~{\AA} (individual seeds:
$4.29$, $2.23$, $2.49$~{\AA}), and no seed passed the 2.0~{\AA} threshold.
An earlier version of this analysis compared this directly to the
original fully rigid result ($2.53$~{\AA}, 22~{\AA} box, Open
Babel-prepared ligand) and read the flexible run as marginally
\emph{worse}. That comparison is invalid: the flexible run differs from
the original rigid run in three ways at once (side-chain flexibility, a
larger 30~{\AA} vs. 22~{\AA} box, and Meeko's automatic removal of
$\sim$400 template-unmatched residues from the receptor), so attributing
the difference to flexibility specifically was not supported. The matched
control (Methods) isolates flexibility alone: fully rigid redocking with
the identical 30~{\AA} box and the identical Meeko-prepared receptor and
ligand, but no residues declared flexible, gives mean symmetry-corrected
RMSD $4.50\pm0.05$~{\AA} -- \emph{worse} than either the original rigid
result or the flexible result. Against this correctly matched baseline,
allowing the five residues to move \emph{improved} the pose from
$4.50$~{\AA} to $3.00$~{\AA}, a real effect in the expected direction; it
simply was not enough to reach the $2.0$~{\AA} threshold. The larger box
size and/or the Meeko receptor-preparation differences, not the choice to
keep side chains rigid, account for most of the difference between the
original $2.53$~{\AA} result and either Meeko-based result -- consistent
with a larger box degrading Vina's search, as a tighter, better-targeted
box gives the optimizer less irrelevant volume to explore. The corrected,
apples-to-apples conclusion is narrower than either the original claim or
its opposite: flexibility helps, modestly, exactly as would be expected if
some of the failure is a rigid-conformer effect -- but it is not enough,
on its own, to rescue the pose, so a genuinely less-supported deposited
pose or a larger-scale conformational change beyond five side chains
remains at least as plausible an explanation as before.

\subsection*{Positive-control validation: the pipeline is not badly
broken, and the CAK finding survives}

The CAK failure rate (13/14, 93\%) is far below AutoDock Vina's
documented self-docking success rate on the 285-complex PDBbind Core Set
(58\%, \citep{zhang2023}), which by itself is compatible with either a
genuine property of the CAK structure series or an undetected problem in
this pipeline's receptor/ligand preparation. The positive control
(Methods) distinguishes these directly: the same, completely unmodified
pipeline, run on 20 objectively selected (first 20 in file order, not
inspected before selection), unrelated CASF-2016 complexes, passed
8/20 (40\%). One structure's automated ligand selection was a genuine
bug caught while reviewing this batch: 3DX1's largest non-excluded
heteroatom group was initially a glycosylation sugar (NAG) rather than
the deposited inhibitor (YHO), because glycans were not on the original
exclusion list; fixed by adding a glycan-code exclusion category and
re-run (the corrected result, $3.29$~{\AA} against the real ligand, still
fails, so this bug did not affect the pass/fail count, but it would have
been a real error in Table~\ref{tab:casf} had it gone unnoticed).

Two comparisons follow from this 40\% figure, and they point in different
directions on different questions. Against the literature's 58\% Vina
baseline, this pipeline's own 40\% is not a significant departure
(one-sided binomial $p=0.08$, $n=20$) -- consistent with a real but
modest performance gap expected from a simpler, generic protocol (a
single fixed 22~{\AA} box regardless of ligand size, Open Babel/Gasteiger
partial charges rather than a more sophisticated charge model, no local
search refinement), not with a broken pipeline. Against \emph{this same
pipeline's own} empirically measured $40\%$ baseline, however, the CAK
result remains a significant outlier: 1/14 passing is far fewer than the
$\sim$5.6 expected at 40\% (one-sided binomial $p=0.008$), while the
original ATR pair's 1/2 is entirely consistent with a 40\% baseline
($p=0.84$). The pipeline, in other words, is doing about what a
generic, non-tuned Vina protocol should be expected to do on an
unrelated, unbiased benchmark -- and the CAK series still fails far more
than that baseline would predict. The 13/14 CAK failure rate, and every
claim in this paper built on it, is treated as established rather than
provisional from this point on.

\subsection*{Using the excluded structure would have skewed the pilot

with one seed for 9L40 against the existing three-seed-averaged 9L4B

coordinates -- but a direct demonstration that had the validation step

\section*{Discussion}

The central finding of this pilot is methodological rather than chemical:
\textbf{a newly deposited, higher-resolution structure is not automatically
a reliable docking receptor, and only an explicit redocking control
establishes this, per structure, before any candidate is trusted.} 9L40 and
9L4B were solved in the same study, yet only one reproducibly passed a
standard accuracy control under an otherwise identical protocol -- and it
is the \emph{lower}-resolution structure (9L4B, 3.20~{\AA}) that passed,
while the higher-resolution one (9L40, 2.87~{\AA}) failed. That inversion
is, in a single sentence, the practical argument for running this control
rather than trusting resolution or recency as a proxy for it. A screening
campaign that skipped this control and used 9L40 by default (e.g., because
it was the first hit in a search, or because it captures the more novel
allosteric site) would have scored every candidate against a receptor
conformation the docking protocol itself cannot reliably reproduce.

We tested, and ruled out, two specific alternative explanations rather than
leaving the failure as an unresolved binary. It is not a symmetry artifact
of naive RMSD: symmetry-corrected RMSD (spyrmsd) narrows the gap from
$2.96$ to $2.53$~{\AA} but 9L40 still fails by a comfortable margin, and the
same pass/fail pattern is exactly reproduced by the two structures that
were never in question (9L4B, 2YM8), which rules out a systematic RMSD
computation error as well. It is not the box reaching into the emptied
allosteric pocket: that pocket's nearest copy is 60.65~{\AA} from the
active site, far outside the 22~{\AA} box used here. The remaining
candidate explanations are a genuinely less well-supported deposited pose
(partially, not fully, consistent with the wwPDB validation data --
comparable Q-score but lower residue inclusion than 9L4B) and a genuine
limitation of rigid-receptor Vina docking against this particular
conformational state (e.g., an induced-fit effect specific to the
dimer-interface-engaged assembly). A third test, run in this round, bears
on the second explanation, though its first analysis was confounded and
had to be corrected (Results): naively comparing the flexible-sidechain
run ($3.00\pm0.92$~{\AA}) to the original rigid result ($2.53$~{\AA})
suggested flexibility made things slightly worse, but that comparison
changed box size and receptor preparation at the same time as
flexibility. Against a properly matched rigid control (same enlarged box,
same Meeko preparation, no flexible residues: $4.50\pm0.05$~{\AA}),
allowing the five residues to move genuinely improved the pose
($4.50\rightarrow3.00$~{\AA}) -- evidence \emph{for} a rigid-conformer
contribution, not against one, the opposite of what the uncorrected
comparison implied. It was still not enough to reach the $2.0$~{\AA}
threshold, so a residual problem remains: either the five chosen residues
are not the whole story (a larger-scale conformational change, e.g.
backbone motion or an induced-fit rearrangement beyond side-chain
reach), or the deposited pose itself is genuinely less well-supported.
This test therefore now argues for a partial rigid-conformer effect
compounding a separate, unresolved problem, rather than against a
rigid-conformer explanation entirely -- a materially different reading
than the one first reported, and reported here because catching and
correcting the confound mattered more than keeping the original
conclusion.

This finding no longer rests on a single failing structure out of two
tested. Extending the identical, independently re-validated pipeline to 14
additional recently deposited cryo-EM structures of an unrelated kinase
system (CAK) found the same failure mode recurring at a similar or higher
rate (13/14, 93\%, vs. the ATR pair's 1/2), so structure-dependent
redocking failure at $\leq$3.2~{\AA} resolution is not an ATR-specific
phenomenon in this sample. What did \emph{not} generalize is the hope,
raised in the previous round of this work, that wwPDB map-model fit
metrics (Q-score, residue inclusion) might explain or predict which
structures fail: across 17 structures, Q-score trends in the wrong
direction (passing structures have \emph{lower}, not higher, mean
Q-score) and residue inclusion shows no relationship at all.

One pair initially seemed to sharpen this point without needing a
correlation statistic, and is worth reporting in full because checking it
changed the conclusion. 8P74 and 8P75 are not, as an earlier draft of this
analysis stated, independent depositions of the same complex bound to the
same inhibitor: their own deposition titles identify them as
\emph{ring-up} and \emph{ring-down} conformational classes of the same
flexible benzylamine substituent (inhibitor ICEC0880, comp\_id X3Z),
computationally separated from the underlying cryo-EM data by the
depositors because the ligand populates both conformations
\citep{cushing2024}. A large RMSD between the two deposited models is
therefore expected by construction, and citing this pair as evidence that
reliability depends on ``the individual deposited coordinate set,
independent of any property of the bound compound'' was wrong -- it
depends here on a property of the compound (conformational heterogeneity)
that this pair was deliberately used to represent. Having caught the
mischaracterization, we tested the specific alternative reading it
suggests -- that Vina's docked pose for 8P74 is not really wrong, but
matches the \emph{other} deposited conformer (8P75's ring-down model) and
was unfairly scored as a failure against the wrong reference -- by
computing symmetry-corrected RMSD between 8P74's docked pose and 8P75's
native ligand directly. It does not: $6.76$~{\AA} from 8P75's native,
statistically indistinguishable from the $6.67$~{\AA} from 8P74's own
native. The docked pose is not a good match to either deposited
conformer. So the corrected reading of this pair is narrower than either
the original claim or its natural alternative: 8P74/8P75 is not usable
evidence for coordinate-set-specific unpredictability (that claim is
withdrawn), but it is also not simply a case of Vina finding a legitimate
alternate pose scored as a failure -- centroid-distance analysis of this
and the other 12 CAK failures (below) points to a third category instead.

Measuring where the docked poses actually land, rather than only their
RMSD to a single reference, adds a useful distinction the RMSD number
alone does not make. Across all 13 CAK failures, the docked ligand's
centroid stays close to its native centroid (1.1--4.1~{\AA}, including
$2.70$~{\AA} for 8P74) despite RMSDs of $5$--$7$~{\AA}: the ligand is not
leaving the pocket, it is adopting a substantially different orientation
\emph{within} it. This is inconsistent with the crudest failure mode a
setup error would produce (a mispositioned or oversized box ejecting the
ligand from the site entirely, which would show large centroid shifts) and
is more consistent with either an orientational degeneracy in this
scaffold's hinge-binding mode or a receptor/ligand-preparation effect that
biases pose orientation without moving the ligand out of the site (for instance,
rigid docking protocols necessarily omit explicit structural waters and subtle
hinge-region backbone adjustments present in cryo-EM models that crystallographic
refinement might otherwise accommodate) -- a
distinction this pilot's scope does not let us resolve further (see
Limitations for what would).

The practical implication of the 13/14 CAK failure rate is nonetheless
substantial if it holds up: it would mean redocking validation is not a
formality that a good enough map-quality score could substitute for, and
that per-structure empirical validation is close to necessary before any
candidate is scored against a newly deposited structure. That rate is,
however, far below what AutoDock Vina is documented to achieve on
curated self-docking benchmarks (Vina's reported self-docking success
rate on the 285-complex PDBbind Core Set is 58\% \citep{zhang2023}), which
raises a real question this pilot cannot dismiss: is 13/14 a genuine
property of this structure series, or a symptom of a receptor/ligand
preparation issue specific to this pipeline's handling of the CAK
complex? A positive-control redocking run of this same, unmodified
pipeline against an independent, externally-curated benchmark set
(Methods, Results) answers this directly: the pipeline's own measured
performance on that benchmark (40\%) is statistically indistinguishable
from the literature Vina baseline (58\%, $p=0.08$), so it is not badly
broken, while the CAK rate remains a significant outlier even against the
pipeline's own 40\% baseline ($p=0.008$). The 13/14 figure is treated as
established, not provisional, on that basis.

This survey remains a sample of convenience regardless of that outcome
(14 of the 17 structures come from one target's fragment-elaboration
series, reachable via this session's network access, not a systematic or
stratified sample of recent kinase depositions; see Limitations).

The pilot docking result is, appropriately for a 35-derivative pilot,
modest rather than dramatic: no generated derivative exceeded the predicted
affinity of the seed compounds, and the best-performing derivatives are
recognizable as close analogs of camonsertib rather than structurally novel
chemotypes. This is consistent with a BRICS recombination run over a
5-compound seed set and a fragment pool of only 22 pieces: the search space
explored is inherently local to the seed chemistry, and a pilot at this
scale should not be expected to surface a structurally distinct chemotype
with superior predicted affinity. What this pilot does show is that the
pipeline behaves sensibly at small scale -- it recovers the seed compound's
own high rank (unsurprising, since camonsertib is the receptor's native
ligand, but a useful sanity check that the docking protocol is not
producing arbitrary rankings) and produces derivatives whose structure-score
relationship is interpretable (small, defined substituent changes at one
position, with correspondingly small score changes) rather than
erratic. Whether a larger seed set, a larger fragment pool, or multiple
recombination rounds would surface derivatives that improve on the seed
compounds is an open question this pilot's scale cannot answer.

\subsection*{Limitations}

This is a small pilot, and its scope was set by what could be executed
directly and verifiably, not by what would be most impressive to report.
Specifically: (1) the multi-structure survey (n=17) that extends the
central reliability finding is a sample of convenience, not a systematic
or stratified one: 14 of the 17 structures come from a single target's
(CAK) fragment-elaboration series, all deposited by the same
research group within a narrow time window, reachable through this
session's network access rather than selected to represent recent kinase
depositions broadly; a single congeneric series sharing one scaffold and
one binding mode is not independent evidence in the way 14 unrelated
targets would be, and the true, structure-independent failure rate could
differ substantially from the 82\% observed here; (1a) the Q-score/residue
inclusion correlation analysis is likewise underpowered by a
pass-group-starved sample (2--3 passing structures of 17) and should be
read as a negative result on this sample, not a general refutation that
any map-quality metric could ever predict redocking reliability;
(1b) the flexible-sidechain redocking test's first, uncorrected comparison
(reported in an earlier draft of this analysis) attributed a difference to
side-chain flexibility that was actually driven mostly by an unmatched box
size and receptor preparation change; the corrected, matched-control
version is reported here, but this stands as a reminder that every
comparison in this pilot needs its confounds enumerated before, not after,
a reviewer catches them, and that the same risk could apply to comparisons
elsewhere in this paper that have not been independently attacked as hard
as this one was; (1c) the CASF-2016 positive control used 20 of 285
complexes, an objectively selected but still small subset, and its own
ligand-of-interest detection (the largest non-excluded heteroatom group)
is a heuristic that produced one confirmed mispick (3DX1: a glycosylation
sugar selected over the real ligand, caught and fixed this round, Results)
and could in principle produce others undetected in the remaining 19; the
$40\%$ pipeline baseline this control establishes should be read with
that heuristic's imperfection in mind, though the specific 8P74/8P75- and
3DX1-type errors this paper has now found and corrected were all caught
by manually reading structure titles and entity lists, not by the
heuristic itself, so this is a real but partial safeguard, not a
complete one; (2) the BRICS recombination
cap (5,000) is combinatorial, not principled, and a larger or differently
seeded run would sample a different, likely larger, region of the
accessible chemical space; (3) the 40-compound pilot library is a small,
deterministic subset of the developability-passed derivatives (ranked by
rule count then molecular weight -- a rule that, incidentally, is stacked
against derivatives outscoring the larger seed compounds), not a
comprehensive screen, and enrichment-style retrieval statistics (ROC-AUC,
enrichment factor) are not meaningful at this scale and are not reported;
(4) no molecular dynamics or MM-GBSA relative energetic estimation was run
-- both require substantially more compute (GPU acceleration for MD at any
informative timescale) than was available, and docking scores alone are
known to correlate imperfectly with binding affinity; (5) no cross-kinase
selectivity panel was run -- this would require additional receptor
structures, each independently redocking-validated by the same standard
applied to the three structures here, which was beyond this pilot's scope;
(6) as throughout, nothing here establishes ATR/CHK1 functional modulation,
inhibition, or activation -- only predicted binding and ranking; (7) the
seed and reference SMILES for prexasertib and ceralasertib were obtained
via web search rather than a structured chemical database query
(PubChem/ChEMBL were not reachable) and, while independently verified
against reported molecular formula and exact mass, should be re-confirmed
against a primary chemical database before further use.

\section*{Conclusions}

Redocking validation -- not resolution, recency, or biological interest --
is what determines whether a deposited structure is a trustworthy docking
receptor. Applied to two contemporaneous ATR structures from the same
study, this control passed the lower-resolution one and failed the
higher-resolution one, a distinction that would not have been visible
without running it, that survives symmetry-corrected RMSD, and that is not
explained by the box reaching an emptied allosteric site. Screened against
the validated structure, a small BRICS-derived sanity-check library
reproduced the expected top rank of the receptor's own native ligand and
generated interpretable, if modest, analogs of it, without yet surfacing a
chemotype that improves on the seed compounds -- a realistic, honestly
reported outcome for a pilot of this scale, and one further reason to treat
larger claims from similarly small screens with caution.

Extending the same pipeline to a larger, if still convenience-sampled, set
of 17 structures across two unrelated kinase targets turned this single
finding into a rate: 14/17 (82\%) overall, and 13/14 (93\%) within the
CAK series specifically, fail redocking at resolutions (1.8--3.2~{\AA})
that would ordinarily be considered more than adequate, and the failure
recurs whether or not the target is ATR. Because that rate is far below
AutoDock Vina's documented performance on curated benchmarks, it was not
taken at face value: the identical, unmodified pipeline was run as a
positive control on 20 objectively selected CASF-2016 complexes,
passing 8/20 (40\%) -- statistically indistinguishable from the
literature's 58\% baseline, ruling out a badly broken pipeline, while the
CAK rate remains a significant outlier even against this pipeline's own
measured 40\% baseline ($p=0.008$). This survey also closed off, rather
than confirmed, the most convenient available explanation for the
original finding: wwPDB Q-score and residue inclusion do not predict
which structures will fail, and in the case of Q-score trend mildly the
wrong way. A flexible-sidechain redocking test -- a low-compute stand-in
for molecular dynamics -- improved the 9L40 pose relative to a properly
matched rigid control, but not enough to rescue it, evidence for a partial
rigid-conformer contribution rather than against one, and a correction to
this paper's own first, confounded reading of that experiment. And
docking the same pilot library against the excluded structure instead of
the validated one produced a substantially different, and by the evidence
above less trustworthy, compound ranking, demonstrating concretely what
validation prevents. One further correction belongs here because it
changed a claim rather than just refining it: an earlier reading of two
CAK structures (8P74/8P75) as independent evidence of coordinate-set-specific
unpredictability did not survive checking their own deposition titles,
which identify them as deliberately separated conformer models of one
flexible ligand -- withdrawn on that basis, without weakening the
survey's overall rate, which does not depend on that pair.

Together these results shift the paper's central claim from ``run this
control, it caught one real problem'' to ``run this control: on the
evidence gathered here, more recently deposited kinase structures fail a
basic redocking check than pass it at a rate this pipeline's own
positive-control performance cannot explain away, no cheap
validation-report metric reliably flags which ones will, and the
difference this makes to a downstream screen is large.'' A systematic,
stratified survey across many independent targets -- not the convenience
sample assembled here -- is the natural next step to turn ``93\% in one
congeneric series'' into a field-general rate. All structures, code, and
intermediate data used in this study are openly available for independent
verification and reuse, including the positive-control validation and
every correction made in response to external review.
